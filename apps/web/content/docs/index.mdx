Open a shadcn dialog and it pops into the middle of the screen. Switch a tab and the panel swaps in a single frame. Add an item to a list and it appears from nowhere. In film, a cut where the subject suddenly jumps to a new place is a jump cut. It's jarring, and it makes you look around for what changed.

Keyframery replaces those jumps with real editing cuts. A dialog grows out of the button that opened it. A tab panel follows the tab a beat later. A sent message flies from the Send button into the thread.

## What you get

- **`<Cuts />`**, one component for your root layout. It finds shadcn's own parts by their `data-slot` names and gives dialogs, alert dialogs, the command menu, sheets, drawers, tabs and toasts their cuts. Eleven more, such as popover, the menus, select and tooltip, are retimed to match.
- **Four helpers** for changes shadcn has no component for: [MatchCut](/docs/helpers/match-cut), [ListCut](/docs/helpers/list-cut), [ValueCut](/docs/helpers/value-cut) and [LoadCut](/docs/helpers/load-cut).
- **Motion theming.** Seven CSS variables and a few attributes let you tune motion the way you tune colour.

## What it doesn't do

It doesn't wrap, fork or replace your components, and nothing in `components/ui` changes. Remove `<Cuts />` and you have stock shadcn again.

Like the rest of shadcn, it's code you own. The CLI copies it into your project.
